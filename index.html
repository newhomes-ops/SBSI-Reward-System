SBSI Reward System

Program Concept

Presented by MTO – Mark Kevin OlivarDate September 30, 2026
Why a Reward System
Recognition today is informal and uneven across departments. Quality lapses are handled case by case.
The gap
  • No common standard for what earns recognition across departments
  • Good work that helps other teams goes unrecognized
  • Certification status and Cost of Quality are not tracked in one place
  • Corrective action varies by lead; no link to reward eligibility
What the program delivers
  • Higher First Pass Yield and lower Cost of Poor Quality
  • Every employee certified on every process they perform
  • Timely, visible recognition, including cross-department support
  • Consistent, documented accountability with due process
  • One auditable view of competency, quality cost and standing
One Program, One Standard
Recognize the behavior, validate the result, reward the impact.
Guiding principle adopted program-wide from JPC-227A (Customer Satisfaction Guidelines)

Fair

Same categories, coin values and eligibility rules in every department. Measures account for role and for factors outside the employee's control.

Evidence-based

Every reward traces to a result, a record and a validator. No soliciting or gaming of surveys or metrics.

Balanced

Rewards sit alongside accountability. A Critical quality event cannot be offset by coins.

What Qualifies
A submission must meet all qualifying tests and none of the exclusions
Qualifies
  • A measurable result against a pillar measure, target or baseline
  • Attributable to the employee (or the team, for team measures)
  • Backed by evidence: case, report, survey or QA record
  • Goes beyond routine expectations of the role
  • Helps another department reach a goal (cross-department)
Does not qualify
  • Normal job duties performed at standard
  • Results driven mainly by factors outside the employee's control
  • Solicited, coached or influenced surveys or metrics
  • Achievements linked to a Critical incident or compliance issue
  • An idea or proposal not yet implemented and measured
  • Already rewarded for the same result under another pillar
How It Works
From achievement to reward in five steps
Submit
Employee, Team Lead or peer completes the SBSI Reward Submission Form with evidence.

Cross-department: the benefiting department nominates.
Validate
Pillar owner or QA confirms the result, the evidence and the eligibility checks.
Approve
Division Lead approves the category and coins. Coins credit only on approval.
Record
Recording (KLC) logs the award in the system of record. The scorecard updates each employee's standing.
Redeem & Recognize
Employee redeems coins (Encashment, Coop, Canteen). Recognition is announced through program campaigns.

Cross-Department Recognition

Rewards may be given across departments when an employee provides significant assistance, support or contribution that helps another team or department achieve its goals or successfully complete an activity or project. Such rewards are subject to review and approval by the respective Division Lead.

Example from the sample log

An Interconnection Specialist helped Permitting clear 40 interconnection-dependent permits ahead of a builder deadline. Nominated by Permitting and approved by the Division Lead: +15 coins.

1 · Nominate

The benefiting department describes the support, the goal and the measurable outcome.

2 · Validate

The benefiting Division Lead confirms the contribution and its impact.

3 · Approve

Coins credit only on approval. The tracker flags any missing approver.

4 · Recognize

The award is recorded and announced to both departments.

Roles & Governance

PTC

Program sponsor. Endorses concept, specification and budget.

Pillar Owners

Maintain criteria and measures. Validate achievements in their pillar.

Division Leads

Approve recognitions, including cross-department. Approve Written Warning and above. Quarterly calibration.

Team Leads & Process Trainers

Nominate. Certify staff. Log quality events and Cost of Quality. Coach.

HR

Due process, labor-law alignment, official records, suspensions.

Program Owner

Keeps the common rules, publishes results, runs quarterly and annual reviews.

Appeals go to the Division Lead within 5 working days of a decision. Voided actions are excluded from scoring but kept for audit.

Risks & Mitigations
RiskMitigation
Perceived unfairness across departmentsOne set of categories, coin values and gates. Quarterly calibration by Division Leads.
Gaming metrics or surveysEvidence and validator on every submission. Eligibility declaration. Solicited results excluded.
Budget overrun on redemptionPeso value per coin, quarterly caps and expiry set within the NTC budget.
Labor-law exposure on attendance and disciplineHR review of corrective action steps. Approved leave must not disqualify an employee.
Double rewards from overlapping measuresEach measure belongs to one pillar only (e.g., FPY under Quality, FCR under CSAT).
Tracking at scale in ExcelHost on SharePoint for co-authoring now. Move to a database or Salesforce as volume grows.
